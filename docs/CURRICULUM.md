# Curriculum: Pee-wee to Architect

Two tracks (kid, adult) that converge at Level 4, plus a kid-only Level 0 for pre-readers. Every level ends with a **level test**
(12 questions, 80% to pass) and a **level game** (70% to pass). Status: `done` = shipped
and validated, `preview` = exemplar content proving the model, `planned` = to be written.

Placement: an adult can take a short quiz per level and start at the first one they miss.
Any level's test can be "tested out" of, with the game still to be played.

## Level 0: Little Huddle (kid, ages 4-7)
Audio-first, no reading, no timers, no test (SPEC 3.1, 4.4, 6.9). Units: The Ball; Two
Teams; Go That Way!; Touchdown! (count to 6); Run, Throw, Catch, Kick; Flags and Tackles;
The Referee (whistle, touchdown and stop signals); Good Sport (taking turns, safety gear).
Every prompt and choice has a recorded audio clip; answers are pictures.

## Level 1: Pee-wee / Fan Starter (done)
Field, yard lines, end zones; offense/defense/special teams; quarters; scoring (TD, extra
point, 2-pt, FG, safety); downs and first downs; punt; snap, handoff, pass, tackle;
turnovers; position names; flag vs tackle; referees and penalties. Adults add: NFL
dimensions, extra point spot (15) and two-point spot (2), down-and-distance, clock
stoppages, play clock, penalty yardage, 7-on-the-line and eligible receivers, touchbacks.

## Level 2: Rookie / Season Ticket (done; see `docs/content-review/level-2.md`)
As written: Kid 9 drills in 3 units (Kicking Plays: kickoff, onside kick, punts and fair
catches; Referees and Rules: signals, common penalties, clock and timeouts; How Plays
Work: run or pass, the offensive line, reading the scoreboard), 58 steps. Adult 9 drills
in 3 units (Formations and Personnel: backfield sets, eligibility, motion and shifts;
Rules That Decide Games: pass interference, protecting players, replay, challenges and
overtime; Special Teams and Calls: tries and kicks, reading a play call, and 11 Game Day
situations for the level game), 65 steps. Kids' scoreboard and timeout rules say "in the
NFL" and point to their own league for youth and flag rules.

Added 2026-10-20 so kids reach Level 4 knowing what it builds on (formation is a
prerequisite of personnel): Kid unit 4, Lining Up (the line of scrimmage, formations,
eligible and ineligible receivers; shotgun, I, singleback, pistol, empty and shifts), and
a Playing Safe drill in Referees and Rules (late hits, roughing the passer, targeting,
illegal contact). Adult drills gained a question each for terms they taught in prose but
never asked about (quarterback, pass play, offside, holding, fair catch...).

Originally planned:
Kids: kicking game (kickoff, onside, return), more penalties and referee signals, the
clock, timeouts, why teams pick run vs pass, offensive line jobs, reading a scoreboard,
basic rules of their own league (flag/youth variants).
Adults: formations by name (I, singleback, shotgun, pistol, empty), eligible numbers and
ineligible-receiver rules, motion and shift rules, pass interference vs contact, targeting
and roughing, challenges and replay, overtime, special-teams anatomy, simple play calls.

## Level 3: Junior Varsity / Armchair Coach (done; see `docs/content-review/level-3.md`)
As written: Kid 9 drills (Routes: go, slant, out, curl, post, corner, deep-and-short
pairs; Man and Zone: man, zone, count the safeties; Runs and Fronts: dive, sweep,
counter, draw, screen, 4-3 and 3-4, 4th down and two-minute basics), 58 steps, 12 taps.
Adult 10 drills (Route Tree: numbering, stems and timing, high-low pairs; Coverage
Basics: man or zone clues, one-high vs two-high shells, leverage; Run Game and Fronts:
five runs, blocking basics, how 4-3 and 3-4 play, the two-minute drill), 57 steps,
12 taps. Every route, front and run is drawn from one set of diagram builders so the
pictures are consistent across tracks.

Added 2026-10-20: Kid unit 4, Clock, Calls and Replay (the play call, the play clock, the
two-minute warning, spike and kneel; replay, the red flag and overtime), plus leverage,
the route tree and pulling guards in the existing kid drills.

Originally planned:
Route tree 0-9 (flat, slant, comeback, curl, out, dig, corner, post, go) drawn on diagrams;
man vs zone coverage in plain language; run plays (dive, sweep, counter, draw, screen);
basic blocking; 4-3 and 3-4 fronts; the 4th-down decision; two-minute drill basics;
reading a defense for the first time ("count the safeties").

## Level 4: Varsity (the tracks converge) (done; see `docs/content-review/level-4.md`)
As written: 5 units, 14 drills, 92 steps, unlocked by passing either Kid L3 or Adult L3.
Personnel and Strength; Fronts and Techniques (gaps, 0-5 techniques, Over, Under, Bear,
Okie, Tite, Wide-9); The Five Coverages (Cover 0-4, each with its pre-snap tells and an
animated reveal, 12 read-the-defense items for the level game); Run Schemes (inside and
outside zone; power, counter, trap, duo); Pass Concepts and Pressure (curl-flat, stick,
snag, smash, mesh, four verticals, Y-cross; sacks, hits, hurries, blitzes, stunts).

Originally planned:
Personnel numbering (11, 12, 21, 10, 13, 22, 20) and formation strength; defensive
fronts (Over, Under, Bear, Okie, Tite, Wide-9, 46) and techniques (0-9); the five base
coverages (Cover 0, 1, 2, 3, 4) and what beats each; zone-run schemes (inside zone,
outside zone) vs gap schemes (power, counter, trap, duo); basic pass concepts (curl-flat,
smash, stick, snag, mesh, four verticals, Y-cross); sacks and pressure vocabulary.

## Level 5: College (done; see `docs/content-review/level-5.md`)

RPOs, zone read, speed/triple option; tempo and no-huddle; tight and wide splits;
pre-snap motion types (jet, orbit, return, across); protections (slide, MIKE ID, hot,

## Level 6: Pro (done; see `docs/content-review/level-6.md`)
As written: 5 units, 10 drills, 59 steps. Every offensive grouping (00 to 23 and jumbo)
and defensive packages taught by defensive-back count (names for six- and seven-back
packages vary, so the course doesn't pick one staff's); Cover 1 robber, 2-man, Cover 2
vs Tampa 2, Cover 3 sky/buzz/cloud, quarters with pattern matching and palms, Cover 6
(12 read-the-defense items); pre-snap reads (safeties, corners, box count with
pictures, motion); the quarterback's progression, option routes and checks; and NFL vs
college rule differences. Deliberately left out pending review: MOD, Cover 9 and Cover 7,
whose names mean different coverages on different staffs.

Originally planned:
**Offensive personnel:** 00, 01, 02, 10, 11, 12, 13, 20, 21, 22, 23 and 6-OL jumbo, with
what each is used for and what defenses answer with. **Defensive packages:** base 4-3 and
3-4, nickel 4-2-5, dime 4-1-6, quarter 3-2-6, big nickel, dollar 3-1-7, goal line 6-2-3 and
5-3-3, with matchups. **Coverages in depth:** Cover 1 (robber, lurk, rat), Cover 2 and
Tampa 2, Cover 3 (sky, cloud, buzz, mable, seam), Cover 4 (quarters, MOD, palms), Cover 6
(quarter-quarter-half), two-man, Cover 9, pattern matching. **Pre-snap reads:** MOFC vs
MOFO, count the box, alignment tells, motion tests (man vs zone). **QB process:**
progressions, hots, sight adjusts, option routes, checking out of a play. **Rules:** the
current NFL rulebook as of the season stamped in the content.

## Level 7: Coordinator (disguise and counters) (done; see `docs/content-review/level-7.md`)
As written: 5 units, 7 drills, 41 steps, 14 disguised read-the-defense items. Rotations
(two-high to Cover 3 or Cover 1, one-high spinning to Cover 2, walkdowns, rotating to
motion); pressure that lies (bluff, sugar, creeper); coverage that lies (trap, fake man,
fake zone, brackets); the offense's answers (motion, tempo, bunch and stack, alerts);
the defense's answers (coverage checks, match rules, calls against tempo); and a mixed
film session. "Tap the tell" questions make the learner find the defender who gives a
disguise away before seeing it play out. Every explanation ends: a tell is a clue, not a
guarantee.

Originally planned:
**Disguise techniques:** two-high shell rotating to Cover 3 (sky/buzz/cloud), one-high
spinning to two-high, late safety rotation, creeping and walkdown, bluff blitz and
sugar blitz, simulated pressure, trap coverage, fake man/fake zone, post-motion
rotation, "bracket" disguises, show-and-bail, mugging the A-gaps and dropping out. **Offense
vs disguise:** motion to ID man/zone, bunch/stack/trips to defeat man, formation tells,
pre-snap shifts, tempo to prevent rotation, reading corners' leverage, post-snap
reads and the "alert" system. **Defense answers:** checks, calls, "match" rules, red/green
coverage-check systems. Every item is a **read-defense** step with a pre-snap and reveal.

## Level 8: Architect (scheme families, analytics, strategy) (done; see `docs/content-review/level-8.md`)
As written: 5 units, 7 drills, 53 steps. Offensive systems (West Coast, Air Coryell,
Erhardt-Perkins, Air Raid, run-and-shoot, wide zone with play-action, spread and RPO,
option and Wing-T roots); defensive systems (two-high light box, pattern-match quarters,
single-high Cover 3, Tampa 2, the 46, Wide-9, 3-3-5) and scheme-vs-scheme matchups;
analytics (expected points and EPA, success rate, pass rate over expected, 4th-down
models, two-point math); game management (clock, home field, weather, roster and cap);
and "The Architect's Game": one fictional game (Harbor City Gulls vs Ridgeview Foxes)
played in order through 11 decisions, which also feed the Game Day gate. System names
that come from coaches or teams are used only as the common labels for the systems.

Originally planned:
**Offenses:** West Coast, Air Raid, Erhardt-Perkins, Coryell (Air Coryell), Shanahan/Kubiak
wide zone and play-action, spread and RPO, Pistol, run-and-shoot, Wing-T and option history.
**Defenses:** Fangio/Vic, Belichick-Saban pattern match, Seattle Cover 3, Tampa 2, 3-3-5
hybrid, Wide-9, 46. **Strategy:** 4th-down models, two-point charts, EPA, success rate,
pass rate over expected, clock management, home-field, weather, roster and cap basics.
**Game:** whole-game decision sims, scheme-vs-scheme matchups.

## Content rules
1. **No licensed material.** No team logos, player names, or copied diagrams. Generic or
   fictional teams; plays are named by common coaching terms.
2. **Rule era is stamped.** Content that depends on a rule (kickoffs, overtime, targeting)
   states the season it reflects and is re-checked each offseason.
3. **Every graded step explains itself.** A wrong answer is the best teaching moment.
4. **Disguise items are defensible.** The key is the most likely play given the tell; the
   explanation says a tell is a clue, not a guarantee.
5. **Kid track:** card text <= 45 words, prompts <= 28 words, read-aloud on, numbered
   jerseys instead of letter labels on tap-the-player quizzes.
6. **Variants:** where flag, youth, high school, college and NFL rules differ, the step says
   which it means.
